# Lecture 14 — AI Innovations, Agents & AGI

## 1. AI Innovation

AI systems continue to develop in areas such as language, image and audio understanding, code generation, reasoning, and the use of external tools. A model is the component that interprets inputs and generates outputs. A product or workflow combines a model with instructions, data, interfaces, and sometimes tools.

Model capabilities, access, pricing, and availability change over time. Comparisons should be based on the task, quality, cost, privacy, and reliability rather than assuming that a newer model is always the best choice.

## 2. AI Agents

An AI agent is a system that can work toward a goal through multiple steps. Depending on its design, it may plan, call tools, inspect results, and decide what to do next. An agent might search an approved data source, summarize findings, update a record, or prepare a message.

An agent still needs clear boundaries. Its tools should have only the access required for the task, its actions should be logged where appropriate, and consequential actions should have human approval. Agent outputs can be wrong, incomplete, or based on flawed data.

## 4. Artificial General Intelligence (AGI)

Artificial General Intelligence refers to a proposed form of AI with broad ability to learn, reason, and adapt across many kinds of tasks, rather than being designed mainly for a narrower set of tasks. There is no single universally accepted threshold for when a system would count as AGI.

Current AI capabilities should not automatically be described as AGI. Systems can be impressive while still making mistakes, lacking dependable understanding, or failing when conditions change. Discussion of AGI therefore includes technical definitions, evaluation, safety, governance, and uncertainty about future progress.

## 5. Applying Agents to Business

A useful business agent starts with a real customer need and a well-defined workflow. It may help discover information, organize it, and prepare a recommendation or draft. The business should measure whether the service saves time or improves access, while protecting personal data and keeping people responsible for important decisions.
